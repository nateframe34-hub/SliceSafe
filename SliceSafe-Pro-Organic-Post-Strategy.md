# SliceSafe Pro — Organic Post Strategy
### Facebook/Instagram Page Content Calendar
### Date: Feb 20, 2026

---

## Goal
Build page authority, engagement history, and audience trust signals with Facebook's algorithm. Organic engagement tells Facebook the page is legitimate and active, which contributes to lower CPMs on paid ads over time.

---

## Rules

- **No product links in organic posts** — kills organic reach
- **No "BUY NOW" energy** — these are content, not ads
- **Ask questions or make statements people want to respond to**
- **Post between 11am-1pm or 6-8pm EST** (peak cooking content times)
- **Respond to every comment within 2-4 hours** — Facebook tracks response rate as a page quality signal
- **Never mention SliceSafe Pro in organic posts** — the goal is engagement and page trust, not sales
- **If someone mentions mandolines organically in the comments**, respond casually but don't pitch
- **Reuse raw images from ad batches** (cutting board, food shots, kitchen scenes) as organic content without text overlay
- **5 posts per week minimum**

## Week 1 Content Calendar

### Post 1 — Monday (Relatable Kitchen Humor)
**Format:** Text post with image
**Image:** The B4_drawer_20min raw cutting board image (messy left vs perfect right) — no text overlay, no branding
**Caption:**
```
Left side: me at 6pm on a Tuesday.
Right side: me when I finally use the right tool.

Which side are you today?
```
**Why this works:** Universal kitchen experience. The image is scroll-stopping on its own. The question drives comments. People will tag friends.

---

### Post 2 — Tuesday (Engagement Poll)
**Format:** Text-only post or simple graphic
**Caption:**
```
Settle this debate:

When a recipe says "thinly sliced" — how thin is thin?

A) Paper thin, translucent
B) About the width of a coin
C) Whatever my knife decides
D) I buy pre-cut and skip the drama

Drop your answer below
```
**Why this works:** Low-effort engagement. Everyone has an opinion. Option C and D are the relatable/funny ones that get shares. Attracts cooking audience without selling anything.

---

### Post 3 — Wednesday (Kitchen Confession)
**Format:** Text post on solid background (Canva — white text on dark background, short and punchy)
**Caption:**
```
Kitchen confession time:

What's the one kitchen tool you bought, used once, and shoved in a drawer?

No judgment. We all have one. Mine was a garlic press.
```
**Why this works:** Directly taps into the Drawer Stuffer sub-avatar territory. People LOVE confessing kitchen failures. This will get tons of comments. Mandoline will 100% come up in the comments naturally — and you don't even have to mention it.

---

### Post 4 — Thursday (Food Content / Trend)
**Format:** Image post — beautiful overhead food photo
**Image:** Find or generate an overhead shot of a beautiful cucumber salad (ties to the trend angle without mentioning the product)
**Caption:**
```
The cucumber salad trend isn't going anywhere.

What's your go-to dressing? We're partial to sesame oil + rice vinegar + chili flakes.
```
**Why this works:** Trend participation gets algorithmic boost. Food photos get saves (which Facebook weighs heavily). The question drives recipe-sharing in comments. Positions the page as a food/cooking page, not just a product page.

---

### Post 5 — Friday (Controversial Take / Debate)
**Format:** Text post
**Caption:**
```
Hot take: A sharp knife is safer than a dull one.

Most kitchen cuts don't come from sharp blades — they come from dull ones that slip.

Agree or disagree?
```
**Why this works:** Polarizing opinion = comments. This is directly from the "Sharp Is Safer" ad angle but framed as a cooking discussion, not a product pitch. People who agree will share it. People who disagree will comment. Both are engagement.

---

### Post 6 — Saturday (Weekend Cooking)
**Format:** Image post
**Image:** Simple photo of prepped vegetables on a cutting board — colorful, appetizing
**Caption:**
```
Saturday meal prep energy.

What's on your cutting board this weekend?
```
**Why this works:** Weekend cooking content. Simple, low-effort engagement driver. People love sharing their meal prep plans.

### Post 7 — Sunday (Story/Reel — if on Instagram)
**Format:** Quick story or carousel
**Content:** "3 vegetables most people are cutting wrong" — short tips about knife angle for onions, how to stabilize a sweet potato, etc.
**Why this works:** Educational content gets saves and shares. Positions the page as a cooking authority. No product mention needed.

## Comment Response Guidelines

- **Someone mentions a mandoline injury:** "Mandolines are the #1 kitchen tool people love to hate. Totally get it." — empathize, don't pitch.
- **Someone asks about a specific tool:** Share a genuine opinion. Be a cooking page, not a sales page.
- **Someone tags a friend:** Like the comment and reply to both. Double engagement.
- **Negative comment:** Respond kindly or ignore. Never argue. Facebook tracks negative interactions.
- **Someone asks about SliceSafe specifically:** Only if they ask directly — "Thanks for asking! We actually make a mandoline that keeps your hands away from the blade. Check the link in our bio if you're curious." Keep it casual and rare.

---

## Measuring Success

Track weekly:
- **Post reach** (should increase over time as page authority builds)
- **Engagement rate** (comments + shares per post)
- **Page followers** (slow growth is fine — quality > quantity)
- **Best-performing post type** (double down on what gets comments)

The goal is NOT sales from organic. The goal is building a page that Facebook trusts, which indirectly lowers CPMs on your paid ads.
