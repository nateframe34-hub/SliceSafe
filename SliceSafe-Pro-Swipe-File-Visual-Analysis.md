## PART 1: Visual Patterns Across ALL 91 Ads

### Pattern 1: Typography Hierarchy Is the #1 Differentiator Between Winners and Losers

**What the winners do:**
- **Primary hook text is MASSIVE** — typically 40-60% of the image height is dedicated to text in the most aggressive formats (Avatar Call Out, Solution Exaggeration, Relatable Hook)
- **Two-level hierarchy only**: one giant hook line + one supporting detail. Never three levels of equally-weighted text
- **Bold/Extra-Bold ONLY for hooks.** Not a single winning ad uses Regular or Light weight for its main headline
- **Contrast is absolute**: white text on dark backgrounds OR dark text on light backgrounds. Zero winning ads use medium-contrast combinations (gray on white, light blue on medium blue, etc.)

**What this means for SliceSafe:**
- Our hooks need to be visually dominant — at least 30% of the image area
- Stop using medium-weight text. Every hook should be Inter Extra-Bold or equivalent
- Two text elements max on any creative: the hook (huge) and the supporting line (small)

### Pattern 2: The "Warm Lifestyle vs. Studio" Split Is Real — And It Maps to Sub-Avatar

**Warm lifestyle backgrounds (real rooms, kitchens, countertops, hands):**
- Huel instant meal cup being eaten by a real hand in a real room
- Dr. Squatch cartoon avatar ads against white (feels native/organic)
- Ridge Wallet product held in a real hand against a desk with keyboard, controller
- Tallow Honey Balm product on a craftsman's workbench with wood shavings
- Sleep/mobility ad: real person on yoga mat in a real bedroom

**Cold studio/clean backgrounds:**
- Joanna Vargas skincare on marble with oranges (looks like an Instagram brand post)
- Creatine gummies on clean blue — simple, but lacks stopping power
- Hair treatment on clean medical layout — informational, not emotional

**The pattern:** Warm lifestyle backgrounds outperform studio shots in ENGAGEMENT-FIRST formats (Pubity Style, Relatable Hook, Feature Annotation). Studio/clean works ONLY for comparison formats (Us vs Them, Strikethrough List, Checklist) where the FORMAT itself is the scroll-stopper, not the image.

**SliceSafe application:**
- For Avatar Call Out, Pubity Style, Feature Annotation: use kitchen counter lifestyle shots with real hands
- For Us vs Them, Strikethrough, Venn Diagram: clean white/light background is FINE because the format carries the scroll-stop
- NEVER use marble. The data already confirmed "sterile product on marble" is dead for us

### Pattern 3: Product Photography — The "In-Use" vs. "Hero" Decision

Three approaches visible across the swipe file:

**1. Product Hero (isolated product, centered):**
- Ridge Wallet 2.0 — 4-quadrant close-up showing features. Works because the PRODUCT itself is visually interesting (metallic, premium texture, new design)
- Creatine Gummies — bag centered on clean background. Forgettable

**2. Product In-Use (someone touching/using it):**
- Huel Instant Meal Cup — hand scooping pasta from it. MOST engaging photo in the entire Huel set
- Ridge Wallet GT — held in hand against phone. Shows scale and context
- Sleep fix — person on mat in bedroom. The BEHAVIOR sells it

**3. Product + Outcome (product alongside the result it creates):**
- "Grab. Heat. Eat." — 5 bowls of prepared meals surrounding the product concept
- Nutella "This is bread / This is breakfast" — the bread IS the outcome

**SliceSafe application:**
- Product Hero: only works if we show the unique vertical design with annotations. Generic "product on counter" = death
- Product In-Use: **THIS IS OUR STRONGEST PLAY.** Hands gripping the top handle, pushing down, vegetables falling into catch tray. This SHOWS the mechanism
- Product + Outcome: show the SliceSafe beside a cutting board of perfectly uniform slices. The slices ARE the proof

### Pattern 4: The Number Rule Is Universal

Across ALL 91 ads, the ones with specific numbers in the hook consistently have cleaner visual hierarchy and feel more credible:

- **"14+ Hours. No Swelling."** — Solution Exaggeration (compression socks)
- **"12-Hour Shift Shoes"** — Relatable Hook (nurses' shoes)
- **"40g Protein"** — Huel (multiple ads lead with this number)
- **"17 meals for $2.65 per meal"** — Huel "This Is Not" template
- **"Grab. Heat. Eat. 7 meals for £2.80 per meal"** — Huel economy play
- **"190 calories. 26 vitamins & minerals. 25g protein."** — Huel Lite
- **"21+ Vitamins & Minerals. 6g Fiber. 1g Net Carb. 0 Sugar."** — Grüns (Stealing Credibility)
- **"$27" (slashed from $36)** — RYZE Specific Pain Point
- **"Only 300 remain!"** — Dr. Squatch scarcity
- **"Dry. Curl. Smooth. Style. All-in-One."** — Lange hair tool (rhythm beats)

**The pattern:** Numbers work in two modes:
1. **Specificity mode:** "14+ hours" or "8 inches" — makes the claim feel measured, not vague
2. **Rhythm mode:** "90 seconds. Perfect slices. 10 fingers." — creates a 3-beat staccato that reads fast

**SliceSafe numbers to deploy:**
- 8 inches (hands above blade distance)
- 90 seconds (total slicing time)
- 60 seconds (cleanup)
- $64.99 (price)
- 10 (fingers you keep)
- 12 inches (tower height)

### Pattern 5: Color Psychology — The 4 Palettes That Work

**Palette 1: Dark background + white/yellow text (BOLD energy)**
- Used by: Ridge Wallet (dark gray/green), Dr. Squatch (black), Huel Black Edition, Uromend, BBS Grow
- Feel: Premium, masculine, confident
- Hook visibility: Maximum — white on dark is the highest contrast possible

**Palette 2: Light/white background + black text (CLEAN energy)**
- Used by: Huel "Old me vs New me," Fodzyme checklist, Nutella Simplified Us vs Them, Avatar Call Out (skincare)
- Feel: Trustworthy, clinical, comparison-friendly
- Hook visibility: High — but feels quieter, more informational

**Palette 3: Brand-color saturated (IDENTITY energy)**
- Used by: Huel (yellow), RYZE (warm brown/gold), Ridge GT (Gulf Racing blue/orange), Grüns (green/yellow)
- Feel: Brand-forward, recognizable on repeat exposures
- Hook visibility: Medium — depends on contrast execution

**Palette 4: Lifestyle photograph + text overlay (NATIVE energy)**
- Used by: Tallow Balm (workshop), Sleep fix (bedroom), Huel meal cup (table), Pubity Style (product in context)
- Feel: Organic, doesn't feel like an ad, feed camouflage
- Hook visibility: Requires text shadow/background strip to ensure readability

**SliceSafe application:**
- **Primary palette: Palette 1 (dark + white/blue text)** — matches our brand blue (#4A90E2), provides maximum hook contrast, signals premium
- **Secondary palette: Palette 2** — for comparison formats (Us vs Them, Checklist, Strikethrough)
- **Test palette: Palette 4** — for native/feed-camo formats (Pubity Style, Feature Annotation) using warm kitchen photos
- **Avoid: Palette 3** — we don't have enough brand equity for color-saturated backgrounds yet

## PART 2: Brand-Specific Visual Breakdowns

### Dr. Squatch (14 ads analyzed)

**Visual identity:**
- Consistent craft-paper packaging as visual anchor across ALL ads
- The sasquatch mascot creates instant recognition at ANY size
- Mix of cartoon illustration ads (Relatable Hook format) and product photography

**Format distribution:**
- 4 offer/bundle ads ("7 Mini Soaps + 3 Free Lip Balms," "Free Hat," "20% Off Sitewide," "Choose Your Scent")
- 2 cartoon problem→solution ads (B.O. exfoliation — both male and female avatar versions)
- 2 scarcity plays ("Only 300 remain!" "Only a few dozen left")
- 2 brand collab/limited edition (Ford F150 & Bronco soaps)
- 2 bundle showcase ads
- 1 sale landing page screenshot

**Key visual takeaways for SliceSafe:**
1. **Consistent visual anchor matters.** The craft-paper box + sasquatch logo = instant recognition. SliceSafe needs its own: the light-blue tower silhouette should appear in EVERY ad, even small
2. **The cartoon/illustration format is a scroll-stopper.** The "My B.O. is terrible" cartoon characters feel native and stop the scroll through NOVELTY — you don't expect an illustrated character in your feed. This is a format we haven't tried
3. **Scarcity with specific numbers:** "Only 300 remain" and "Only a few dozen left" — they use SPECIFIC small quantities. More believable than "Limited Time"
4. **Offer stacking is their primary conversion play.** Most Dr. Squatch ads lead with the DEAL, not the product story. Multiple free items + bundle pricing. Our $64.99 (was $99.95) + free catch tray + free cleaning brush parallels this approach but we haven't visualized the value stack
5. **The cartoon problem→solution format applied to SliceSafe:**
   - Character 1 (worried face): "I want to make that cucumber salad but mandolines scare me. What do I do?"
   - Product response: "Your hands grip the top. The blade is inside. You can't reach it."
   - This is essentially the Indirect Avatar format but using illustration instead of photography

### Huel (22 ads analyzed)

**Visual identity:**
- Black + yellow is DOMINANT. Nearly every ad uses this palette
- Product is always shown in-use or freshly prepared, rarely isolated on its own
- Typography: clean sans-serif, BIG primary number/claim, smaller supporting facts

**Format distribution:**
- 5 "This Is Not [Product]" reframe ads — their signature format
- 4 economy/value ads (price per meal breakdowns with crossed-out prices)
- 3 feature annotation ads (product with floating callout pills)
- 3 lifestyle/unboxing ads (real rooms, real hands)
- 2 "Old me vs New me" comparison ads
- 2 offer/free-gift ads (T-shirt + shaker)
- 2 breakthrough/new mechanism ads ("breakthrough for calorie counting")
- 1 3-beat rhythm ad ("Grab. Heat. Eat.")

**Key visual takeaways for SliceSafe:**
1. **"This Is Not" is their single most repeated format.** They run it in English, Polish, German — it's clearly a proven winner they've scaled globally. The hook structure "This is a [desired outcome]. Not a [category name]." directly maps to: "This is a kitchen tool. Not a mandoline." or "This is meal prep in 90 seconds. Not a mandoline."
2. **Feature annotation on lifestyle photos is their highest-engagement visual style.** The Huel Instant Meal Cup with floating "22g protein," "Ready in 5 minutes," "Goodbye lunch prep" labels is the MOST native-feeling ad in their set. Real hand, real food, real moment + feature callouts. SliceSafe equivalent: real hands pushing down on the handle, with floating callouts: "Hands 8 inches above blade," "90-second slices," "Dishwasher safe"
3. **Economy framing works through CROSSED-OUT prices.** "£2.80 per meal" with a line through it is a visual pattern they use repeatedly. Shows value without feeling cheap. We could use: "~~$99.95~~ $64.99" as a visual element
4. **"Old me vs New me" is an Us vs Them variant targeting the SAME person** (before/after, not two products). This is gentler than product-vs-product comparison. SliceSafe version: "Old me: Holding my breath while I slice" / "New me: Push down. Walk away. Done."
5. **The 3-beat rhythm headline is everywhere.** "Grab. Heat. Eat." — three single words, periods after each. This is EXACTLY what works for our B3_scroll_90sec winner ("90 seconds. Perfect slices. 10 fingers."). Huel confirms this format works at scale

### Ridge Wallet (21 ads analyzed)

**Visual identity:**
- Product-forward photography — the wallet IS the visual
- Premium materials and finishes are the hero (metal textures, anodized colors)
- Dark, moody backgrounds with dramatic lighting (desk setups, workshop aesthetics)
- Strong use of collection/ecosystem shots (wallet + keycase + pen + phone case)

**Format distribution:**
- 5 new product launch ads ("Introducing Ridge 2.0," "Introducing Hyper Lime," "Gold Standard")
- 4 feature annotation ads (icons + text callouts around product)
- 3 "Built to [quality]" assertion ads
- 3 collection/ecosystem ads ("Back in Stock" kit layout)
- 2 collab/identity ads (GT Collection racing theme, Collegiate wallets)
- 2 lifestyle/in-hand ads
- 2 premium material hero shots

**Key visual takeaways for SliceSafe:**
1. **Ridge proves that a SINGLE product can sustain massive ad variety through ANGLES, not SKUs.** They sell one core product (a metal wallet) but generate creative variety through: new materials (gold, lime), new features (MagSafe, tracker), new contexts (school, racing, everyday carry), new photography angles (macro, in-hand, flat-lay kit). SliceSafe can do the same: kitchen contexts (meal prep, dinner party, solo cooking), food variety (cucumbers, potatoes, onions, fruit), mechanism angles (top view, side view, close-up of enclosed blade)
2. **The "Built to [quality]" assertion format is powerful for premium pricing.** "Built Like a Bullet, Performs Without Bulk" and "Built to Outperform" justify Ridge's $100+ price point without apologizing for it. SliceSafe equivalent: "Built to Keep Your Hands Safe" or "Designed So You Never Touch the Blade"
3. **Icon rows for features work better than bullet lists.** Ridge uses small icons (feather for "lighter," shield for "warranty," card for "capacity") with one-line labels beneath. 4 icons in a row at the bottom of the ad. This is more scannable than text bullets. SliceSafe could use: blade icon = "Enclosed blade," hand icon = "Hands-on-top design," clock icon = "90-second prep," dollar icon = "$64.99"
4. **The flat-lay kit shot creates perceived ecosystem value.** The "Hyper Lime Collection" shows wallet + keycase + pen + phone case + AirTag holder arranged in a tray. This makes one product feel like a SYSTEM. SliceSafe version: mandoline + catch tray + cleaning brush + recipe card arranged on a kitchen counter. Makes $64.99 feel like a kit, not a single item
5. **Color-driven campaign launches stop the scroll through visual novelty.** The Hyper Lime (neon yellow/green) and GT (Gulf Racing blue/orange) collections are visually impossible to ignore. Our light-blue is already distinctive — we should lean INTO the color, making it the scroll-stopping visual element rather than hiding it in context

## PART 3: Unlabeled Media Ads — Hidden Patterns

### The 78 Unlabeled Ads Reveal 6 Additional Visual Execution Patterns:

**1. The "Premium Objection Crusher" (Tallow Balm)**
"Not the cheapest tallow balm, BUT DEFINITELY THE BEST..."
- Owns the objection IN THE HOOK before the viewer can think it
- Product sits in a warm, craftsman workshop setting (wood shavings, chisels)
- The ellipsis creates an open loop — you want to know what makes it the best
- **SliceSafe version:** "Not the cheapest mandoline. The one that can't cut you."

**2. The "Stop [Embarrassing Behavior]" Pattern (Uromend)**
"STOP EXPLAINING Your Bathroom Trips"
- "STOP" in red creates urgency/pattern interrupt
- Calls out a behavior the viewer is currently DOING
- Product floats alongside the behavioral context (toilet paper ribbon)
- **SliceSafe version:** "STOP white-knuckling your mandoline." or "STOP holding your breath when you slice."

**3. The "Category Reframe" (Fodzyme vs Lactaid)**
"More than Lactose" — checklist showing Fodzyme checks 3 boxes vs Lactaid's 1
- Side-by-side checklist comparison against the MARKET LEADER
- Doesn't attack the competitor — just shows the gap
- Clean, scannable, takes 2 seconds to understand
- **SliceSafe version:** Checklist comparing "Traditional Mandoline" vs "SliceSafe Pro" — Exposed blade (X vs check), Guard included (check vs check), Hands near blade (X vs check), Enclosed blade (X vs check), Catch tray (X vs check). The visual WEIGHT of checks tells the story

**4. The "Staccato Rhythm" Pattern (Lange hair tool)**
"Dry. Curl. Smooth. Style. All-in-One."
- Single-word beats create a fast-reading, punchy cadence
- Product laid out flat with all attachments visible
- The periods after each word FORCE the reader to pause on each beat
- **SliceSafe version:** "Slice. Push. Done." or "Safe. Fast. Uniform. Every time."

**5. The "Fix X by Fixing Y" Mechanism Reveal (Sleep/Mobility)**
"FIX YOUR SLEEP BY FIXING YOUR NERVOUS SYSTEM"
- Reveals a non-obvious mechanism — the REAL cause isn't what you think
- The person is shown DOING the mechanism (yoga pose in bedroom)
- Supporting text: "5 minutes of active mobility per day resets the system that controls your sleep"
- **SliceSafe version:** "Fix your mandoline fear by fixing your mandoline." — reveal that the problem was never YOUR skill, it was the DESIGN of traditional mandolines

**6. The "Daily [Benefit] Without the [Drawback]" (Creatine Gummies)**
"Daily Creatine. Without the powder."
- Two sentences. First names the benefit. Second removes the objection
- Ultra-clean, minimal visual — product bag + two gummies
- Third-party tested badge as trust signal
- **SliceSafe version:** "Paper-thin slices. Without the fear." or "Perfect cuts. Without the risk."

## PART 4: The Master Visual Execution Checklist

Based on everything analyzed above, every SliceSafe static ad should pass these visual checks before launching:

### Hook Text
- [ ] Is the hook 48px+ Bold/Extra-Bold minimum?
- [ ] Is it 2-7 words maximum?
- [ ] Does it use assertion format (NOT a question)?
- [ ] Is it high-contrast (white on dark OR dark on light — never medium)?
- [ ] Does it occupy the top 15-30% of the image?
- [ ] If using numbers, are they SPECIFIC (8 inches, not "several inches")?

### Visual Structure
- [ ] Is there a clear 2-level text hierarchy (hook + support, nothing else)?
- [ ] Can you understand the ad in under 2 seconds WITHOUT reading body copy?
- [ ] Is the product shown in-use OR annotated? (Never just sitting there)
- [ ] Does the image show HANDS? (Hands = mechanism proof)
- [ ] Is the light-blue mandoline tower visible and recognizable?

### Format Compliance
- [ ] Does this pass the "is this a sterile product on marble?" kill check? (If yes → KILL)
- [ ] Does the format match the sub-avatar? (Comparison formats for Fence Sitter, Avatar Call Out for Drawer Stuffer, Feature Annotation for Scarred Chef, etc.)
- [ ] Is the copy doing SHOW vs TELL? (Behaviors, not features)
- [ ] Does it use at least 2 FRAMES? (SAFE, EASY, NEW, FAST)

### Color & Background
- [ ] Primary ads: dark background + white text + brand blue (#4A90E2) accent?
- [ ] Comparison ads: clean white/light background?
- [ ] Native/feed-camo ads: warm kitchen lifestyle photo?
- [ ] NO marble, NO sterile white studio, NO medium-contrast combinations?

### Trust Elements (at least 1 per ad)
- [ ] Price anchor ($99.95 → $64.99)?
- [ ] Social proof (review quote, star rating)?
- [ ] Specificity (exact measurements, exact times)?
- [ ] Risk reversal (guarantee, free shipping)?

## PART 5: Top 10 Visual Executions to Build for SliceSafe (Priority Order)

Based on everything in this analysis + the performance data, these are the 10 specific visual executions with the highest probability of converting:

### 1. Avatar Call Out — Drawer Stuffer
**Layout:** Giant text top 40%: "YOUR MANDOLINE IS IN THE DRAWER." Below: warm kitchen photo of SliceSafe standing on counter with hands pushing handle. Small text: review from verified buyer about finally using it.
**Why:** Avatar Call Out is Tier 1 format. Drawer Stuffer is our proven winning sub-avatar. Warm lifestyle background matches data. This is the safest bet.

### 2. Feature Annotation — In-Use Photo
**Layout:** Real hands pushing down on SliceSafe handle from above. 3 floating callout pills: "Hands 8 inches above blade" (arrow to hands), "Blade enclosed inside" (arrow to mid-section), "Catch tray included" (arrow to base). Dark vignette background.
**Why:** Huel's most engaging format. SHOWS the mechanism instead of claiming it. Human hands = trust. Three callouts = scannable.

### 3. Minimal Us vs Them — Competitor Comparison
**Layout:** Left side: traditional flat mandoline with red callout labels ("Exposed blade," "Hands near blade," "Guard nobody uses," "Flimsy plastic"). Right side: SliceSafe with simple text: "None of that." + SliceSafe Pro logo.
**Why:** Directly from the Carepod swipe file example. Reverses traditional comparison by only showing THEIR problems. Clean, devastatingly simple.

### 4. "This Is Not" Reframe — Fence Sitter
**Layout:** SliceSafe product in warm kitchen setting. Large text: "This is a kitchen tool. Not a mandoline." Supporting text below: unique numbers (8 inches, 90 seconds, $64.99). Brand blue price pill.
**Why:** Huel's single most-repeated winning format. Sidesteps self-categorization ("mandoline" triggers fear). Creates curiosity gap.

### 5. Solution Exaggeration — Scarred Chef
**Layout:** Dark background. Giant white text: "8 INCHES. ZERO FEAR." Below: side-profile photo of SliceSafe showing the distance between top handle and enclosed blade. Small supporting feature icons at bottom.
**Why:** Compression socks "14+ Hours. No Swelling" format. Number + bold claim. Specific measurement makes it tangible.

### 6. 3-Beat Rhythm — Scroll Stopper
**Layout:** Dark background. Stacked text, each line its own beat: "90 seconds." / "Perfect slices." / "10 fingers." Below: SliceSafe product photo. Price pill bottom right.
**Why:** Our PROVEN winner format (B3_scroll_90sec at $28.79 CPA). Confirmed by Huel's "Grab. Heat. Eat." and Lange's "Dry. Curl. Smooth. Style." Scale what already works.

### 7. Old Me vs New Me — Drawer Stuffer
**Layout:** Split image. Left "Old me": hand hovering nervously over flat mandoline, red X marks: "Holding my breath," "Guard keeps slipping," "Hands too close." Right "New me": hands confidently pushing SliceSafe from top, green checks: "Push down from the top," "Blade fully enclosed," "Done in 90 seconds."
**Why:** Huel's "Old me vs New me" applied to our product. Same person, two experiences. Empathetic, not competitive.

### 8. "Stop [Behavior]" — Scarred Chef
**Layout:** Dark background. Red "STOP" text + white continuation: "STOP holding your breath when you slice." Below: SliceSafe with the Hands-On-Top handle prominently visible. "Hands grip the top. Blade stays inside. $64.99"
**Why:** Uromend's pattern-interrupt format. "STOP" in contrasting color = instant scroll halt. Calls out a behavior ONLY mandoline users recognize = self-selecting filter.

### 9. Value Stack / Free Ad — Broad
**Layout:** SliceSafe product center. Floating callout bubbles around it, each with value: "Catch tray ($12.99 value)," "Cleaning brush ($11.99 value)," "Enclosed blade design," "90-second uniform slices." Top text: "Everything you need. Nothing you're afraid of." Price: "$64.99 (was $99.95 — SAVE 35%)"
**Why:** Dr. Squatch's primary conversion format. Stack perceived value. Our included accessories become "free gifts." Makes $64.99 feel like a deal.

### 10. Checklist Comparison — Fence Sitter
**Layout:** Clean white background. Two columns. Left: "Traditional Mandoline" with X marks. Right: "SliceSafe Pro" with checkmarks. Rows: Exposed blade / Enclosed blade, Hands near blade / Hands on top, Guard that slides off / No guard needed, Flat and wobbly / Stands upright, Fear / Confidence. Products at bottom of each column.
**Why:** Fodzyme vs Lactaid format. 2-second comprehension. The VISUAL WEIGHT of checkmarks vs X marks tells the story before the viewer reads a word.

## PART 6: Key Takeaways — The 5 Rules These 91 Ads Teach Us

1. **The hook IS the ad.** In static advertising, you have the hook text and you have the supporting visual. The hook must be readable at phone-thumb size (1080px wide, viewed on a 6" screen). If someone can't read your hook while scrolling at speed, nothing else matters.

2. **Show the mechanism, never just claim it.** Every winning ad in this swipe file either SHOWS the product in action (Huel meal cup being eaten, Ridge wallet in hand, compression socks on legs) or uses a visual format that IMPLIES action (Us vs Them, Before/After, Checklist). None of our losing ads showed the Hands-On-Top mechanism in action.

3. **Pick ONE emotional register per ad.** Dr. Squatch ads are either funny OR premium OR deal-focused — never all three. Huel ads are either educational OR aspirational OR value-focused. Mixing registers creates confusion. Each SliceSafe ad should be clearly: safety/relief, speed/efficiency, value/deal, OR social proof/trust.

4. **Consistency beats novelty after the first scroll-stop.** Ridge Wallet and Dr. Squatch have ads that are INSTANTLY recognizable as theirs even without the logo. This comes from consistent color palette, consistent product angle, consistent typography. We need our light-blue tower + Inter font + brand blue (#4A90E2) to become recognizable.

5. **Every premium product in this swipe file justifies its price through SPECIFICITY, not claims.** Ridge doesn't say "high quality." They say "10% lighter, oil-filled logos, enhanced modularity, built for life warranty." Huel doesn't say "healthy." They say "40g protein, 26 vitamins, 400 calories, $2.65 per meal." SliceSafe shouldn't say "safe." We should say "8 inches between your hands and the blade, enclosed stainless steel V-blade, push-down mechanism, stands 12 inches tall."
