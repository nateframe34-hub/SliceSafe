Market Desires:
What Is Desire: 
The Force that makes advertising work
Strong feelings of wants or needs of people 
Desire is the demand that already exists and is flowing through the market
You Can Not create desire; you can only channel it 

Types Of Desire In the Market:
2 Main types of desire 
Permanent Desire
Health
Wealth
Relationships
Desires that never leave us 
Trending Desire 
Crypto
Valentines day
AI
Desires that come and go

Desire Strength:
The Strength of your desire will determine the success of your ad 
Your goal is to find the dominant desire based on:
Intensity → Minor Headache vs. Severe Migraine
Scope → The total number of people who share this desire

Market Sophistication:
Lesson Example:
When all of the products look the same, there is no clear or obvious choice… They all seem to be the same, so you just pick one 
But when a well-branded or new mechanism/product is introduced, it automatically grabs attention and becomes the more attractive choice 

What Is Market Sophistication:
Market sophistication is the level of knowledge your market has of your product/services 
How many similar products has your market been told about before?
How many similar products have they tried before 
Market Sophistication Stages:
Stage 1:
Definition:
First to market, aka the Market has never seen your product
Goal:
Be simple, Be Direct
Action:
Name the claim and prove it works



Stage 2:
Definition:
Market is starting to see it
Goal:
Enlarge the successful claim of competitors and one-up your competitors 
Action:
Make bigger and stronger claims. 
 
Stage 3:
Definition:
Market Has Hear All The Claims
Goal:
Introduce a new mechanism
Action:
Focus on HOW the product delivers the SAME claim in a NEW and better way
 
Stage 4:
Definition:
Market Now Knows The Mechanisms
Goal:
Enlarging your mechanism
Action:
 Make it easier, faster, solve it better, overcome limitations
 
 
Stage 5:
Definition:
 Market No Longer Believes You
Goal:
Focus your advertising on identification with your prospect
Action:
Show the market the version of themselves they want to become, selling the persona of using your products 








How To Determine Market Sophistication:
Analyze your product and the problems it solves 
Identify all of the competitors selling the same product 
Identify all of the businesses solving the same problem

Create a Market Sophistication Foreplay board 
During competitor research, leverage foreplay to build your own “Boxes” and see which ones resonate with you and how you can attack an angle that the market has not seen before


How To Find Winning Products:
Myth-Busting:
There is no “Winning Product.” Any Product can be a “Winner”
Spam Test Is BS
If your spam testing is relying on LUCK
Instant Success
Gurus tell you success is immediate, but building a brand takes time and persistence 

Mindset: What To Expect:
Expect To Fail
Realistically, when you first start testing, you’re going to suck 
The more reps, the better you get 
Chase High Intent Reps, Not Perfection
Most beginners don’t make it in Ecom because they try to be perfect 
Intent - Track Everything You Do
Track every product and creative you test, and list why you want to test 
Feedback Loops
As long as you keep testing, you are guaranteed to succeed
The more you succeed, the easier it will become to succeed 
Success
As you start to learn and get better, you put in enough hours, and your product hit rate goes up
Success may take longer than you think, but if you continue, it is guaranteed 




Product Criteria:
AOV and profit margin
Aim for an AOV of $60+ after bundles to ensure profitability 
Min 3x COGS + Shipping, ideally more. This will give you a huge advantage in  scaling 
Trend Awareness
Keep an eye on emerging trends 
Do a quick Google Trends search on the niche and trend. Make sure it’s not trending down
Product Weight 
Avoid heavy items 
Shoebox Rule 

What Even Is A Winning Product?:
A winning product is a product that satisfies a mass desire 
Any product can work, but some will be more difficult than others 

What Affects How Easy and Hard It Is To Make A Product Work:
Magnitude Of Desire
Weak Desire Example: I’d like my desk to look more organized 
Desktop organizers ($15-$30)
Cable management clips ($10)
Decorative pen holders ($20)
Strong Desire Example: I need to lose 30 pounds before my wedding in 3 months
High-ticket weight loss coaching ($2000-$5000)
Specialty meal delivery services ($300-$500/month)
Personal Training ($1000-$3000)
Medical weight loss procedures ($5000-$15000)












How Do You Deal With Competition/Saturation?
Saturation = Big audience. Don’t Be Afraid Of Competition 
This is the PITFALL of most of the copy-paste gurus 
You Don’t Have To Start With Stock Or Customized Product 
Starting out, it’s a bad idea to buy upfront stock
2 Ways You Can Stand Out 
Either innovate the product 
Either you have a new product/mechanism that solves an existing problem better
Or innovate on the avatar 
Target an underserved avatar

You Don’t Need An Untapped Market Or Game-Changing Desire To Win:
Case Study 1: Shilajit (New Mechanism)
A year or two ago, when Shilajit was just in the resin form, it was a bunch of Level 1 and Level 2 sophistication claims
Brands came in with a new form of delivery (mechanism) to reset sophistication 
Other solutions to succeed would be framing the product as “Ours Is The Best Due To These Lab Test”
Case Study 2: Stanley (New Avatar)
Stanley Cups were originally made for construction workers to keep their drinks hot.
Sophistication reset
Originally an old Market (Stage 5) (Construction Workers)
“Keeps ice 2 hours longer than YETI
“Double-wall vacuum insulation with proper lining”
New Market (Stage 1) (Gen Z)
“Your emotional support water bottle”
“The cup that makes you drink more water.”











Case Study 3: TrueClassic (New Avatar + Mechanism)
All the other brands were targeting hypebeast (Supreme), gymbros (Gymshark), bargain hunters (Hanes) and athletic dudes (Cutts). TrueClassic targeted an underserved avatar, the “Dad Bod” (Age 30-55)
Traditional T-shirt marketing 
“Athletic fit” (Bad For Dad Bods)
Models With 6 Packs 
Brand Hype 
TrueClassic’s Avatar-Specific Marketing:
Regular guys with realistic bodies 
“Before/after” in the same moment (Just changing shirts) 
Relates to the Avatar
Traditional T-Shirt Claims (Stage 5)
Everyone Claiming “Softest,” “Highest Quality,” “Best Fit”
TrueClassic’s Claims:
First to acknowledge this avatar exists 
First to offer a solution 
No competition speaking to this pain point 
Mechanism Innovation:
Body-optimizing fit (Hides gut, enhances shoulders)
