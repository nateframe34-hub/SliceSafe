## Strategy: Why These 3 Ads

Batch 2 tests the FOOD-FIRST / OUTCOME angle — "Look at this beautiful dish, the tool is in the background."
Batch 3 tests the SAFETY / MECHANISM angle — "This product is fundamentally different from every mandoline you've seen."

These are different buyer motivations:
- **Outcome-driven** (Batch 2): "I want to make that dish" → clicks to learn more
- **Problem-driven** (Batch 3): "I'm scared of mandolines" → clicks because this one is different

By testing both angles, we learn which MOTIVATION drives more purchases at $64.99.

### What Batch 3 Tests
| Ad | Format | Angle | Avatar | Psychology |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| #14 | Split-screen reframe | Category disruption | Scared Home Cook + Injury Survivor | Contrast Effect, Framing, Anchoring |
| #15 | Mechanism hero | Direct safety claim | Injury Survivor + Scared Home Cook | Loss Aversion, Present Bias, Regret Aversion |
| #16 | Food-first + safety twist | Trend + humor + safety | TikTok Trend Follower + Injury Survivor | Bandwagon Effect, Pattern Interrupt, Availability Heuristic |

### Why This Mix Is Strategic
- **Format diversity**: Meta's Andromeda algorithm rewards creative diversity — 3 different formats means the algorithm can find different audiences for each
- **Avatar coverage**: Batch 2 targeted Efficiency Seekers + Trend Followers. Batch 3 adds Scared Home Cooks + Injury Survivors.
- **Angle isolation**: If Batch 3 outperforms Batch 2, we know fear/safety messaging converts better than food aspiration. If Batch 2 wins, we double down on food-first.

## AD #14: "This is a mandoline. This is the mandoline, redesigned."

**Ad name in Meta:** `reframe_redesigned`
**Format:** Split-screen "This is / This is" reframe (Nutella-style)
**Target avatars:** Scared Home Cook + Injury Survivor

**Why this format:** The Nutella "This is bread. This is breakfast." format works because it uses minimal words to create maximum contrast. It reframes how you see an entire product category. For mandolines, the reframe is: dangerous tool → safe tool. The split-screen makes the difference VISIBLE in under 2 seconds.

**Psychology applied:**
- **Contrast Effect** — Left side (scary) makes right side (safe) look dramatically better
- **Framing Effect** — Same product category, completely different perception
- **Anchoring** — The dangerous mandoline anchors expectations, so the safe one feels revolutionary
- **Loss Aversion** — Implies "you've been risking your fingers this whole time"

**Primary Text (Meta ad copy):**
```
Every mandoline on the market puts your fingers inches from the blade. This one doesn't.

SliceSafe Pro stands upright. You press from the top. Your hands never go near the blade — 8 inches of distance, by design.

The mandoline, redesigned.

## AD #15: "8 inches between your hands and the blade."

**Ad name in Meta:** `mechanism_8inches`
**Format:** Mechanism hero / Bold statement
**Target avatars:** Injury Survivor + Scared Home Cook

**Why this format:** This is the simplest, most direct ad in the batch. ONE message. ONE image. The mechanism IS the sell. Research shows "Your hands never go near the blade" is the single most powerful line in your arsenal — it maps directly to the Hands-On-Top design AND addresses the #1 fear in the category. This ad communicates that in 2 seconds.

**Psychology applied:**
- **Loss Aversion** — Frames what you'd lose (fingers) without this product. Losses feel 2x as painful as gains.
- **Present Bias** — "8 inches right now" is an immediate, tangible safety benefit, not a future promise
- **Regret Aversion** — People who've been cut by a mandoline will feel the regret and want to prevent it from ever happening again
- **Specificity** — "8 inches" is concrete and measurable. Not "safe" or "protected" — a specific number.

**Primary Text (Meta ad copy):**
```
8 inches. That's the distance between your hands and the blade.

No cut-resistant gloves. No flimsy plastic guard. Your hands press from the top — they physically cannot reach the blade.

That's not a feature. That's the whole point.

SliceSafe Pro™ Mandoline — $64.99 (was $99.95)
🔗 velaine.shop
```
*First 125 chars: "8 inches. That's the distance between your hands and the blade." = 64 chars — ultra-front-loaded, specific, creates immediate curiosity.*

## AD #16: "Made zucchini noodles from scratch. Kept all 10 fingers."

**Ad name in Meta:** `zoodles_10fingers`
**Format:** Food-first image + safety-humor text (hybrid)
**Target avatars:** TikTok Trend Follower + Injury Survivor

**Why this format:** This ad takes the PROVEN format (food-first image) and tests a completely DIFFERENT text angle (safety humor instead of aspiration). If both Batch 2 (aspiration text) and this ad (safety text) convert on food-first images, we know the IMAGE FORMAT works regardless of text angle. If only one converts, we know which TEXT ANGLE resonates. Smart testing = isolating variables.

The dish is zucchini noodles (julienned strips), which:
- Is trendy (health-conscious, low-carb audience)
- Requires a mandoline (relevant product use case)
- Is visually different from all Batch 2 dishes (no overlap)

**Psychology applied:**
- **Bandwagon Effect** — Zucchini noodles are a recognized food trend. People see others making them.
- **Pattern Interrupt** — They expect another food caption ("So delicious!"). Instead they get safety humor. The unexpected angle stops the scroll.
- **Availability Heuristic** — The mandoline injury trend makes danger feel recent and common. "Kept all 10 fingers" activates that memory.
- **Humor / Pratfall Effect** — Self-deprecating humor ("I'm surprised I still have my fingers") is more relatable than polished brand copy.

```
Made zucchini noodles from scratch. Kept all 10 fingers.

Loaded the zucchini, pressed down from the top, and perfect julienne strips came out the bottom. My hands were on top the whole time — nowhere near the blade.

## What We Learn From Batch 3

### If AD #14 (reframe) wins:
→ People respond to CATEGORY DISRUPTION. Build more comparison/reframe ads. The "redesigned" positioning is your hook.

### If AD #15 (mechanism) wins:
→ People respond to DIRECT SAFETY CLAIMS. The mechanism sells itself. Build more product-hero and feature-focused ads.

### If AD #16 (zoodles + safety humor) wins:
→ The food-first format works AND safety humor outperforms aspiration. Make every food-first ad include a safety-humor angle.

### If Batch 2 food-first outperforms all of Batch 3:
→ The OUTCOME (beautiful food) is the stronger motivator. Double down on food-first with new dishes. Safety messaging works better in the primary text below the image, not as the scroll-stopper.

### Kill/scale criteria (same as before):
- Kill if: CTR < 1.5% after 3-5 days
- Iterate if: CTR > 2% but no conversions → test different text overlays
- Scale if: ROAS > 1.5x → increase budget 20% every 3 days

## Copy Review Notes (Skills Applied)

### From paid-ads:
- All primary text front-loads the hook in first 125 characters (before "See more")
- PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve) framework used for AD #14
- BAB (Before-After-Bridge) framework used for AD #15
- Social/trend hook used for AD #16
- Each ad tests ONE concept/angle (testing hierarchy: concept > hook > visual > copy > CTA)

### From marketing-psychology:
- AD #14: Contrast Effect + Framing Effect + Anchoring (old → new)
- AD #15: Loss Aversion + Present Bias + Regret Aversion (specific number = concrete)
- AD #16: Bandwagon Effect + Pattern Interrupt + Availability Heuristic + Pratfall Effect (humor)
- Price anchor ($64.99 was $99.95) in all primary text = Anchoring Effect
- First-person voice = Similarity Bias / Unity Principle (sounds like "one of us")

### From copywriting:
- Clarity over cleverness in all 3 ads — no puns that obscure meaning
- Specificity: "8 inches" not "safe distance," "10 fingers" not "safely"
- One idea per ad: #14 = redesigned, #15 = distance, #16 = humor + safety
- Customer language used throughout: "fingers near the blade," "cut-resistant gloves," "holding my breath"
- All headlines under 40 chars, all descriptions under 30 chars
