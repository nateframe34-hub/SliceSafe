### Psychology Principles Applied Throughout
| Principle | How We Use It |
|---|---|
| **Loss Aversion** | "You're losing 20 minutes every time you chop by hand" — losses hurt 2x more than gains |
| **Contrast Effect** | Old flat mandoline vs SliceSafe side-by-side — makes improvement vivid |
| **Anchoring** | Lead with specific numbers (8 inches, 90 seconds, $99.95 → $64.99) |
| **Social Proof** | Real UGC quotes as hooks — "real people said this" |
| **Status-Quo Bias** | Reframe inaction: "it's not your fault, it's the design" |
| **Unity / Identity** | "For people who actually cook" — shared identity drives purchase |
| **Present Bias** | Immediate outcomes: "Start slicing today" not "You'll save time eventually" |
| **Regret Aversion** | "30-day guarantee" in every body copy — removes regret fear |

## Production Rules (Updated from Week 2)

### Nano Banana Pro:
- **Attach SliceSafe Pro product reference photos** to every generation that includes the mandoline
- If the mandoline looks wrong: generate the scene WITHOUT the mandoline, then composite a real product photo in Canva
- **MANDATORY mandoline description in every prompt:** "a light-blue vertical standing mandoline slicer — it stands upright like a small tower about 12 inches tall with a wide ergonomic pressing handle on top, a transparent food hopper in the middle showing the stainless steel blade inside, and a dark-gray rubber non-slip base at the bottom. A clear plastic catch tray sits at the base underneath the blade."
- Never reference other images in prompts — use concrete composition instructions
- All prompts target **4:5 aspect ratio** (1080 x 1350 px)

### Canva:
- All text overlays added in Canva — NEVER baked into AI image
- Standard font: **Inter** (or Helvetica/SF Pro as fallback)
- Price tag pill: #4A90E2 background, white text, Inter 16-18px
- Export: PNG, highest quality, 1080 x 1350 px
- Text should cover no more than 20% of image area

### NEW Rules from Week 2 Data:
1. **Assertion hooks ONLY.** Never open with a question. Both UGC winners and both static winners used declarative statements.
2. **Numbers stop the scroll.** B3_scroll_90sec's numbers-led hook outperformed all other formats on efficiency. Use specific numbers whenever possible.
3. **3-beat rhythm for hooks.** [Number/Benefit]. [Quality]. [Safety punchline]. This structure is proven.
4. **Warm lifestyle environments > studio shots.** B4_drawer_finally's warm kitchen converted. The winning UGC videos were in messy kitchens and outdoor settings.
5. **Max 3 feature points per creative.** UGC winners covered 3 key points. The loser covered 7+.
6. **Relief/discovery energy > educational energy.** "Finally." (relief) converted. "Do you remember..." (educational setup) didn't.
7. **Kill at 35+ impressions with 0 clicks.** B3_scroll_ugc proved this threshold.

## Canva Design System (Quick Reference — same as Week 2)

| Hook text (bold/numbers) | Inter | 44-72px | Bold | White or #1A1A1A |
| Punchline accent | Inter | **40-54px** | **Bold** | #4A90E2 (brand blue) |
| Native/camouflage caption | Inter | 22-26px | Regular/Semi-bold | White #FFFFFF |

**VISUAL AUDIT UPDATE:** Hook text on lifestyle photos bumped from 30-36px Semi-bold → 38-54px Bold. Swipe file winners use 48-80px Bold/Black. Previous standard was too small and got lost on busy backgrounds. Native/camouflage captions intentionally small — they're meant to look like social captions, not ad headlines.
| Stat/danger text | Inter | 40-80px | Bold | #FF4444 (red) |

| Subtext | Inter | 20-24px | Regular | #666666 |
