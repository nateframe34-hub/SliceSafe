SECTION 5: The PRODUCT
As I said: simplicity is king. When I'm looking for products to test, I'm not looking for something nobody's ever seen. I'm looking for the exact opposite.
PROVEN DEMAND IS EVERYTHING
I don't sell anything that isn't already proven. Period.
The way I validate this is simple: I want to see at least one competitor doing at least $300K per month selling something similar. That's it. One competitor. Doing real volume.
"But Mark, how do I know how much someone else is doing?"
Great question. Here are a few methods: 
Method 1: SimilarWeb
Go to similarweb.com, get the Chrome extension, and look at their traffic volume.You can generally tell how well someone is doing based purely on traffic. Ideally looking for 300k+ visits per month and trending up.
Method 2: GetHookd (Ad Spy)
Look for competitors that have 100+ active ads in their Meta ad library. If someone is running that many ads, they're spending serious money which means they're making serious money.
Method 3: Google Keyword Tracking
Go to ads.google.com, open a free account, and search for keywords related to your product. You want to see at least 30,000 monthly searches for the main term.
Method 4: Helium 10 (Amazon)
Check Amazon sales volume for similar products to estimate market demand.
The method is actually less important than the principle: sell something that's already proven to work.

THE MARGIN RULES
Good margins are not optional. They're the difference between a business and a hobby that loses money.
Two rules. Both must be met.
Rule #1: At least $30 gross margin
Take your selling price, subtract your product cost including shipping. That number needs to be at least $30.
Rule #2: At least 3x your COGS (including shipping)
If your product costs $40 to manufacture and ship, you need to sell it for at least $120.
Example:
Selling price: $100
COGS (product + shipping): $40
Gross margin: $60
But $100 is only 2.5x of $40
If you can sell that same product for $120:
Gross margin: $80
3x COGS: $120 = 3x of $40
Both rules met. Now you have a product worth testing.

PURPLE OCEAN THEORY
This is one of the most important concepts in this entire guide.
You've probably heard of Blue Ocean and Red Ocean theory. Quick refresher:
Red Ocean = Extremely saturated market. Competitors everywhere. Margins get compressed down to nothing. Think selling a generic carnivore supplement with zero differentiation. You and 500 other people all had the same "brilliant" idea.
Blue Ocean = Zero competition. Sounds great, right? Except zero competition usually means zero demand. You invented a product nobody wants. Congratulations.
Both of these suck.
Purple Ocean is where you want to be. You enter a proven, red-ish market, but you carve out a hyper-specific demographic within it.
Example 1: Primal Queen
The carnivore supplement market is red. But Primal Queen sells carnivore supplements specifically for women who are postpartum or perimenopausal. Hyper-specific. Purple ocean.
Example 2: Nootropics for Entrepreneurs
I personally bought nootropics back in 2018, not because they were the best ones on Amazon, but because they were marketed as nootropics specifically for entrepreneurs. Purple ocean. That company exited.
If you try to sell to everybody, you're really selling to nobody.

Side Note: DROPSHIPPING IS NOT A BUSINESS MODEL
I need to say this clearly: dropshipping is a fulfillment model, not a business model.
It's literally just shipping a product from your supplier to your customer without a warehouse in the middle. Walmart does it. Apple does it. Home Depot does it. It's just a way to fulfill orders.
You should be using dropshipping to validate that you can actually sell a product. Once it's validated, you build a real brand around it.
There's a reason why most dropshippers' income looks like an EKG up, down, up, down. It's because they're not building anything sustainable. They find a product, milk it, it dies, and they start over.
This guide is called branded dropshipping for a reason. Build a brand.

CREATING YOUR CREATIVES
Two approaches. Use both.
Method 1: Swipe What's Working (80% of your creative volume)
Go to GetHookd or the Meta Ad Library. Find ads that are clearly working for competitors not just in your niche, but across all niches. Find the best-performing ones. Remake them for your product.
This is not copying. You're taking a proven concept and making it your own.
Method 2: Original Concepts from Research (20%)
Use the gold in your foundational docs. There are emotional triggers, pain points, and messaging angles in that research that nobody else has turned into ads yet. Create original concepts based on what you found.
80/20 split. Swipes and originals. Test both.

THE LAUNCH SETUP
Super basic. On purpose.
Campaign Structure:
1 CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization)
3 ad sets inside (each one = a different messaging concept)
2-6 creatives inside each ad set (all variations of that one concept)
Set minimum daily spend per ad set
Budget:
$50–$250/day total for the CBO
Never go under $50/day total; anything less is too slow for meaningful data
Targeting:
Start with the Big 4: US, Canada, Australia, UK
Run broad let Facebook's algorithm do the targeting for you
Don't overthink audiences. Creatives ARE your targeting in 2026.
If you want to use ASC (Advantage Shopping Campaigns) instead of CBOs, that works too. I've tested both extensively. It doesn't make that big of a difference.


THE PIXEL TRUTH
Let me save you from a rabbit hole: the pixel is overrated.
The pixel is just a piece of JavaScript code that captures data and sends it to Facebook's servers. That's it. It doesn't "hold" data. It doesn't "learn." The ad account optimizes around the data on the server not on the pixel itself.
Don't worry about "seasoning" your pixel. Don't worry about "confusing" it by testing different products. Focus your energy on what actually matters: the ad account and the creatives.

SECTION 8: READING THE DATA DON'T BE EMOTIONAL
You've launched. Ads are running. Now what?

MEASURE IN SPEND, NOT DAYS
Most people ask "how many days should I test?" Wrong question.
Measure in total ad spend, not time. You want at least $200–$300 in data before you make a real decision about whether something is working or not.
At $150/day, that's about 2 days. At $50/day, that's about 4-6 days. The spend is what matters, not the calendar.
That said, I like to run for at least 3 days because Facebook does have good days and bad days. But spend is the primary metric.


WHEN TO CUT
Simple rules:
Cut immediately if: An individual ad has spent past your breakeven CPA without a single sale. Dead. Kill it.
Cut if: An ad has dropped past your breakeven ROAS and is trending downward. Not going to magically recover. Kill it.
Don't cut if: You haven't spent enough to reach statistical significance yet. Give it the $200-$300 it needs.

50-70% OF YOUR CREATIVES WILL FAIL
Read that again. 50-70% of the creatives you test will not work. And that's totally okay. That's completely normal.
The game is not about having every ad work. The game is about finding the ones that DO work the ones that cracked the winning message and doubling down on them.
When something works:
Create more variations of that concept
Test it in different formats (image → video or vice versa)
Scale the budget on that ad set
Use that messaging on your landing page
When something doesn't work:
Kill it
Don't be emotional about it
Move on to the next concept
Regardless of how hard you worked on something, you're just going to read the data as is.
