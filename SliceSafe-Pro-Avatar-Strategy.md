## PART 1: Product Understanding (Two-Product Reality)

### Physical Product:
- Light-blue vertical standing mandoline slicer, 12" tall
- Ergonomic pressing handle on top, transparent food hopper, V-blade, rubber non-slip base, clear catch tray
- Includes catch tray with lid ($12.99 value) + cleaning brush ($11.99 value)
- $64.99 (was $99.95) — SAVE 35%

### Functional Product (What it DOES for people):
| Performance | What it delivers |
|---|---|
| Safety | Keeps hands 8+ inches from the blade during use AND cleanup |
| Speed | Turns 20 minutes of chopping into 3 minutes of slicing |
| Precision | Paper-thin to thick-cut, restaurant-quality uniform slices every time |
| Simplicity | No knife skills required. Drop, press, done. |
| Easy cleanup | Open rinse design + brush = 10-second cleanup, fingers never near blade |

### Product Mechanism: "Hands-On-Top" Design
**How it creates the result:** Your hands grip the top handle and push straight down. The blade is enclosed in the mid-section. Your hands physically CANNOT reach the blade during normal use. Safety is built into the design itself — not dependent on a guard, gloves, or discipline.

**Why this mechanism matters:** This is a GENUINE new mechanism. Traditional mandolines = flat blade, hands move toward it, guard exists but nobody uses it. SliceSafe Pro = vertical design, hands stay on top, blade enclosed below. This isn't a "better guard." It's a fundamentally different approach.

**In Origins language:** This mechanism provides NEW HOPE in a Stage 3-4 sophisticated market. It's not "another safe mandoline." It's "the first mandoline where your hands physically can't reach the blade."

## PART 2: Market Sophistication Assessment

### Stage: 3-4 (Saturation → Mechanism Competition)

**Evidence:**
- Multiple "safe mandoline" products exist (Dash Safe Slice, Benriner, OXO, etc.)
- Cut-resistant gloves are a common "solution" ($8-15)
- Guards are standard on all mandolines — and nobody uses them
- Consumers are educated: they know mandolines are dangerous and have heard "safer" claims before
- Logan Moffitt promotes Dash Safe Slice to millions — that mechanism is now a known quantity

**The Avatar-Market Sophistication Gap:**
Our previous ads FAILED because we communicated at Stage 1-2 level:
- "The only mandoline where your hands never go near the blade" → This is a CLAIM without naming the mechanism
- Food-first images → People clicked for the FOOD, not because they believed our safety claim
- Generic "safe" positioning → Didn't break through the sophistication

**Required Strategic Response: New Mechanism**
We need to NAME the mechanism ("Hands-On-Top"), SHOW it (hands on top, blade below, 8 inches), and EXPLAIN why previous mechanisms failed (guards don't work because the design forces your hands toward the blade).

**Secondary Response: New Information**
Reframe the problem: "The reason you got cut wasn't carelessness. It was the design. Every traditional mandoline points the blade at your hands."

## PART 7: Testing Strategy (Origins Framework)

### Method: Marksman (for all 5 batches)
Per Origins: "For images, use the Marksman method. Test the best angle for each sub-avatar in a 3:2:2."
- 3 creatives = 3 different angles (or 3 variations of angle execution)
- 2 body copies = 2 different primary text approaches
- 2 headlines = 2 different headlines
- All go into Meta as one test

### Why Marksman for everything:
- We have NO direction yet — zero winning angles identified
- We need to find which sub-avatar + angle Meta favors
- Images are best tested with Marksman (per Origins: "Whenever testing images, use the marksman method")
- Once we find a direction (after 3-5 days of data), we switch to Sniper with video

- **Batch 1:** Monday (Sub-Avatar Showdown — test all 3 sub-avatars)

After each batch has 48 hours of data:
1. Which sub-avatar got the most spend? (Meta's signal of relevance)
