## Winner Analysis — What the 3 Converters Have In Common

### B3_scroll_90sec — $28.79 CPA (best in account)
- Numbers-forward rhythm: "90 seconds. Perfect slices. 10 fingers."
- Problem-implicit, not product-first. "10 fingers" is the punchline — names the fear without naming the product.
- The copy does the work, not the image.
- Scroll Stopper sub-avatar.
- **Caveat:** Only 284 impressions and 2 LPVs. This purchase could be a lucky break at small sample size. We can't draw definitive conclusions from 1 conversion on this little data. But it's a signal.

### cucumber_salad — $189.56 CPA
- **FOOD is the hero.** The cucumber salad is front and center — the mandoline is in the background, out of focus. This is the Batch 2 strategy doc's core finding: "Food-first — the dish is the hero, not the product."
- Native Instagram food photo aesthetic — doesn't look like an ad.
- Primary text does the selling: "Made the viral cucumber salad. Easier than I expected." Problem-first ("viral cucumber salad" = trend), product is the bridge.
- Highest all-time CTR with real volume: 2.44% across 1,312 impressions.
- 23 LPVs but only 1 purchase = 4.3% LPV-to-purchase rate.
- CPA is too high ($189.56) but it PROVED the format works.

### B4_drawer_finally — $207.83 CPA
- "Finally." — one of the strongest emotional hooks in the account. Speaks directly to the Drawer Stuffer's years of avoidance.
- Warm kitchen lifestyle scene, not a sterile product shot. Active cooking, not posed.
- The algorithm gave this the most spend by far ($207.83) — meaning it had the strongest early engagement signals.
- 35 LPVs but only 1 purchase = 2.9% LPV-to-purchase rate.
- CPA is way too high, but it got the most traffic to the PDP.

### What the 3 Winners Share
1. **Emotional or aspirational hook** — "Finally." / "Made the viral cucumber salad" / "90 seconds. Perfect slices. 10 fingers." All three create a feeling before showing the product.
2. **Not sterile product shots.** cucumber_salad is food-first. B4_drawer_finally is warm kitchen lifestyle. B3_scroll_90sec is product but with punchy numbers doing the heavy lifting.
3. **Primary text or copy is doing real work.** The text isn't just "product name + claim." Each one tells a micro-story or creates a rhythm.

## Feedback Loops — Losers With $20+ Spend

### B1_drawer_design — $122.81, 0 purchases
**Concept:** "Your mandoline is in the drawer. That's not your fault — it's the design." Split-screen: old mandoline in messy drawer vs SliceSafe on counter.

**Why it didn't work:**
1. **The split-screen comparison format is the most recognized ad format on Meta.** The moment you see "left side = bad, right side = good," you know it's an ad. Zero curiosity gap. Compare to cucumber_salad where you don't even realize it's an ad until you've already engaged.
2. **Brand bias.** Assumes the viewer (a) owns a mandoline AND (b) keeps it in a drawer AND (c) feels some way about that. That's a narrow intersection.
3. **"That's not your fault — it's the design" is a weak resolution.** It tries to reframe guilt, but the Drawer Stuffer isn't feeling guilt — they're feeling annoyance or indifference. The hook doesn't match the emotion.
4. **Interesting data point:** 17 LPVs, 1 ATC, 0 purchases. Someone added to cart but didn't buy. The PDP didn't close, OR they compared prices, OR it was an accidental click.

**Diagnostic checklist:**
- Brand bias? **YES** — assumes mandoline ownership + drawer storage
- Product-first? **YES** — split-screen = instant ad recognition
- Mechanism shown? **NO** — clean product shot doesn't explain HOW it's different
- 2-second clarity? **Partial** — the split-screen concept is clear, but WHY this one stays on the counter isn't

---

### B1_scarred_mechanism — $86.59, 0 purchases
**Concept:** "I have a scar from my old mandoline. This one can't do that." Product photo on marble countertop.

**Why it didn't work:**
1. **Claim without proof.** "This one can't do that" — says who? The image is a product on marble. Nothing in the visual demonstrates WHY it can't cut you.
2. **Visual-text mismatch.** The text is visceral and emotional ("I have a scar"), but the image is sterile and clinical (marble, clean lighting, no person). The registers clash.
3. **Lowest CTR of any ad with significant spend: 1.12%.** People are scrolling right past. The product-on-marble look isn't stopping anyone.
4. **Highest CPC: $12.37.** The few people who do click cost a lot, meaning the algorithm is struggling to find the right audience.

**Diagnostic checklist:**
- Brand bias? **YES** — assumes mandoline scar/injury experience
- Product-first? **YES** — "This one" = product is the subject of line 2
- Mechanism shown? **NO** — marble product shot shows nothing about how it keeps you safe
- 2-second clarity? **NO** — need to read both lines + infer the safety mechanism

---

### cucumber_salad_v2 — $84.34, 0 purchases
**Concept:** Iteration on the winning cucumber_salad concept — food-first with different styling.

**Why it didn't work:**
1. **Highest CTR in the account at 3.28%.** People are stopping AND clicking. 12 clicks, 13 LPVs — solid engagement.
2. **But 0 purchases from 13 LPVs.** The landing page didn't close, OR the people clicking aren't purchase-intent — they're curious about the food, not the product.
3. **CPM is $230.44 — the second highest in the account.** Meta is charging a premium to show this ad.
4. **The original cucumber_salad converted at $189.56 CPA with 23 LPVs (4.3% conversion). cucumber_salad_v2 has 13 LPVs and 0 conversions.** At 4.3%, you'd expect ~0.56 purchases from 13 LPVs. So 0 is within statistical noise — this might not actually be a worse ad, just unlucky at small sample size.

**Diagnostic checklist:**
- Brand bias? **NO** — food-first, anyone can relate
- Product-first? **NO** — food is the hero
- Mechanism shown? **Partially** — product in background implies the tool made this possible
- 2-second clarity? **YES** — you see food, you want the food

**Note:** This one's failure may not be a creative problem at all. The $230 CPM is the red flag — it's getting shown to expensive audiences.

### split_screen_produce_tax — $52.13, 0 purchases
**Concept:** AD #6 "Pre-Cut Produce Tax" — infographic cost comparison. Pre-cut store veggies ($6.99/week = $363/year) vs SliceSafe ($64.99 one-time).

**Why it didn't work:**
1. **Infographic + math = processing burden.** This requires the viewer to read numbers, do mental math, and accept the savings frame. Too much cognitive work for someone scrolling at speed.
2. **The "save money on pre-cut veggies" angle targets the wrong motivation.** People don't buy mandolines to save money — they buy them for speed, food quality, or safety.
3. **The format (flat-lay comparison with text math) looks like an ad.** Zero feed camouflage.

**Diagnostic checklist:**
- Brand bias? **NO** — everyone buys groceries
- Product-first? **NO** — leads with cost savings angle
- Mechanism shown? **NO** — it's a cost comparison, not a product demo
- 2-second clarity? **NO** — requires reading math to understand the message

**Winner comparison:** All 3 winners appeal to emotion (fear, aspiration, relief). This ad appeals to logic/math. Logic doesn't stop thumbs.

### stat_bomb_6pm — $28.33, 0 purchases
**Concept:** AD #4 "The Stat Bomb" — "At 6pm, your hands are tired and your focus is gone. This is designed for exactly that moment."

**Why it didn't work:**
1. **Lowest CPM in the entire account: $59.27. Lowest CPC: $3.15.** Cheapest traffic by far.
2. **But 0 purchases from 9 LPVs.** The traffic was cheap but not converting.
3. **The "6pm fatigue" angle is too abstract.** "Your hands are tired" — not a felt problem while scrolling. Compare to "10 fingers" (visceral) or "Made the viral cucumber salad" (aspirational).
4. **This is the IC trap all over again.** Low CPM + low CPC = cheap eyeballs, but cheap eyeballs ≠ buyers.

**Diagnostic checklist:**
- Brand bias? **NO** — everyone has tired evenings
- Product-first? **Partial** — "This is designed for exactly that moment" = product as resolution
- Mechanism shown? **NO** — lifestyle image of tired parent, no product mechanism visible
- 2-second clarity? **YES** — the emotion is clear, but it doesn't connect to "buy this mandoline"

**This proves:** Cost efficiency ≠ purchase intent. Don't chase CPMs.

## 4 Patterns Across All Losers

### Pattern 1: "Sterile product on marble/white + text claim" is a dead format

**Ads using "product on marble/white + text claim" format:**
- B1_scarred_mechanism: $86.59, 0 purchases, **1.12% CTR**
- B2_scarred_noguard: $28.81, 0 purchases (2.52% CTR but only 2 LPVs)
- B5_mech_handstop: $22.66, 0 purchases, **1.17% CTR**

**Ads using other formats that converted:**
- cucumber_salad (native food post): 1 purchase, **2.44% CTR**
- B4_drawer_finally (warm lifestyle): 1 purchase, **1.66% CTR**
- B3_scroll_90sec (numbers rhythm on product): 1 purchase, **1.76% CTR**

**The refinement:** It's not just "product + headline" that fails. It's specifically **sterile product shot + claim text**. When the product is in a warm living context (B4), or the food is the hero (cucumber_salad), or the copy rhythm is so strong it overrides the format (B3), the ad can work. The key variable: **does this look like someone's real life, or does it look like a brand trying to sell me something?**

Swipe file confirmation: ComfortWear, Ridge Wallet, Huel — none use clean marble + sans-serif text claim. That's the actual dead format.

### Pattern 2: The mechanism is never SHOWN in the losers

Of the 8 losers with $20+ spend, how many actually SHOW the hands-on-top mechanism in the image?
- B1_drawer_design: **NO**
- B1_scarred_mechanism: **NO**
- cucumber_salad_v2: **NO**
- split_screen_produce_tax: **NO**
- stat_bomb_6pm: **NO**
- B2_scarred_noguard: **NO**
- B5_mech_handstop: **YES** — annotation arrows show zones but no hands in use
- pico_dices_too: **NO**

**1 out of 8 losers shows the mechanism, and it does so with labels, not with actual human hands.** Compare to B13_fence_native which shows a person's hands pressing down on the handle, clearly 8 inches above the blade. That's PROOF, not a diagram.

### Pattern 3: Cheap traffic ≠ purchases

| Ad | CPM | CPC | Purchases |
|---|---|---|---|
| stat_bomb_6pm | **$59.27** (cheapest) | **$3.15** (cheapest) | 0 |
| B4_drawer_finally | $88.40 | $5.33 | 1 |
| B3_scroll_90sec | $101.37 | $5.76 | 1 |
| cucumber_salad | **$144.48** | $5.92 | 1 |

The cheapest traffic didn't convert. The most expensive CPM (cucumber_salad) did convert. **Don't chase CPMs.** Matches the IC campaign lesson.

### Pattern 4: High CTR without conversion = wrong audience or weak intent

| Ad | CTR | LPVs | Purchases |
|---|---|---|---|
| cucumber_salad_v2 | **3.28%** | 13 | 0 |
| B2_scarred_noguard | **2.52%** | 2 | 0 |
| cucumber_salad | 2.44% | 23 | **1** |
| ugc_no_gloves | 2.11% | 1 | 0 |
| stat_bomb_6pm | 1.88% | 9 | 0 |

Food-first format attracts food-curious people, not necessarily buyers. Food-first needs stronger product connection in the primary text to filter for purchase intent.

## Pre-Launch Checklist (Use Before Creating Any New Ad)

| # | Question | What the data proves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is this a sterile product shot on marble/white with text claim? | Every ad in this format failed (B1_scarred, B2_noguard, B5_handstop). Kill this format. |
| 2 | Does this show the mechanism with real human hands? | 0 out of 8 losers showed hands-on-top in action. B13_fence_native does. |
| 3 | Am I leading with problem/emotion or product/claim? | All 3 winners lead with emotion or outcome. All losers lead with product claims. |
| 4 | Would someone mistake this for organic content? | cucumber_salad (native food post) had the highest CTR. B5_mech_handstop (infographic) had the lowest. |
| 5 | Is the copy doing heavy lifting, or am I relying on the image? | B3_scroll_90sec proves strong copy can carry even a basic product image. B1_scarred_mechanism proves weak copy can't be saved by a clean product shot. |
| 6 | Am I chasing cheap CPMs? | stat_bomb_6pm had the cheapest traffic in the account and 0 purchases. Don't optimize for cheap. |

---

## Core Takeaway

**Your copy is often excellent but it's trapped in a format that people scroll past.** The mechanism copy from B5_mech_handstop, the emotional hook from B4_drawer_finally, the safety humor from B3_scroll_90sec — these are winning messages. They just need to be in formats that stop thumbs. The new swipe file formats (Avatar Call Out, Strikethrough, Us vs Them, Feed Camouflage, Venn Diagram) are the right direction.
